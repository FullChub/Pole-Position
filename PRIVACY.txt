Pole Position Privacy Policy
Last updated: 10 October 2026

Pole Position is developed by David Leonardi. This policy explains the privacy update to gameplay, Challenge Mode and optional play statistics. The developer-operated services do not create player accounts, build player profiles, or use a persistent installation identifier.

Information on your device

Pole Position stores gameplay results, performance history, preferences, coaching history and secret achievement progress on your device. Solo gameplay does not require Game Center. Your complete local performance and coaching history is not uploaded to the developer's services.

Game Center and achievements

If you sign in to Apple's Game Center, Pole Position uses Apple's services to submit scores, display rankings and player profiles, and report and retrieve earned achievements. These Game Center features remain associated with your Apple Game Center account. Your Game Center name, profile and scores may be visible to other players according to Apple's services and your settings.

The developer's Challenge Mode and optional statistics services do not receive your Game Center name, player identifier, identity proof or Apple Account password. Apple handles Game Center information under its own privacy terms: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/.

Challenge Mode lobbies

Private two-player lobbies use a developer-operated service hosted on Cloudflare. No account or Game Center sign-in is required. When you create or join a lobby, the app creates a new random private access key for your place in that lobby. A key is used only for that lobby, and the server stores a hash scoped to that lobby. The keys are kept on your device so you can resume your lobbies. They are not a global player or device identifier and are not used to link different lobbies to one person.

The service stores the lobby title, the nicknames chosen for that lobby, invitation code, game mode, attempts, submitted scores, results and the times needed to enforce deadlines. This enables invitations, attempt limits, retry recovery and deciding the winner. The other participant can see the lobby title, your lobby nickname, best score, attempt count and result. Use a nickname rather than your real name or contact details. Do not put personal information in a lobby title.

Invitation links let another person join an available lobby. Share them only with the person you want to play. Your private lobby access key is not included in an invitation link. There is no account-based lookup of your lobbies; losing the local access keys can make them inaccessible to you.

Lobby records, their attempts and access-key hashes become eligible for deletion 30 days after the lobby was created. Scheduled cleanup removes expired records. A lobby still connects the two participants' attempts and scores within that game; the service does not connect that activity to a real-world identity, Game Center account or persistent device identifier, or use it to build a history across lobbies.

Optional aggregate play statistics

Share Anonymous Run Totals is off by default. You can choose to enable it in Settings → Privacy, and turn it off at any time without affecting gameplay. Consent to the earlier statistics system does not enable this replacement automatically.

When enabled, the app sends counts of started and completed solo runs for Lights Out and Shift Point, grouped by the UTC day the run started. It does not send a name, Game Center identifier, installation identifier, session identifier, exact play time, individual run identifier, reaction time or shift score. Challenge Mode and native Game Center challenges are excluded.

Each batch has a fresh random report ID used only to prevent the same batch being counted twice during a retry. A later batch uses an unrelated ID. The service adds the counters to shared daily totals and shared five-minute receipt-time totals. It retains a hash for each retry receipt and a report-scoped payload hash for at most the report's current and following UTC day, then removes them in scheduled cleanup. These receipts are not used to identify players or connect different batches.

The private developer dashboard shows aggregate run counts, not unique players, unique devices or who is online. Aggregate totals are retained for 30 days. Turning sharing off stops further reports, cancels pending activity uploads and clears unsent counters. Reports already merged into shared totals cannot be separated by player because there is no player identifier. Offline counters are held briefly in app memory and can be lost when the app closes.

Hosting, security and retention

Cloudflare provides hosting, storage and network security. Online requests necessarily involve network information such as an IP address. The services use short-lived edge rate limits to limit abuse. IP addresses are not stored in the application's databases or associated with stored lobby or play-statistics records. Worker request observability is disabled, and application error handling does not log request bodies, access keys or IP addresses. Cloudflare processes infrastructure information under its own terms and privacy policy: https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/.

Scheduled deletion can take place after the stated eligibility time. Cloudflare D1's automatic recovery history on the free plan can retain deleted database data for up to seven additional days. Recovery data is not used for analytics or to restore player profiles. If a database is restored, expired and retired data must be removed again before the service resumes.

The earlier services used a Game Center identifier and display name for competitions and an installation identifier for play activity. The privacy update removes that earlier application database data rather than transferring it into the replacement system. Older app versions cannot create account-linked competitions, and their legacy statistics requests are discarded without storing their contents. Cloudflare's recovery-history period also applies to the removed legacy records.

Advertising and tracking

Pole Position does not include advertising, sell player data, or use this information for advertising tracking across apps or websites. It does not include a third-party analytics SDK. The optional aggregate statistics are used to understand use of the two games and improve the app; they are not combined with other datasets to identify players.

On-device AI

On supported iPhones, Race Engineer uses Apple's on-device Foundation Models to provide feedback based on app-calculated gameplay statistics. Pole Position does not send gameplay or coaching data to an external AI service. App-calculated coaching remains available when the on-device model is unavailable.

Exports and backups

You can choose to export results or back up and restore app data using the system file interface. Files are saved to the location you select. If you choose a cloud storage provider or share a file, that provider or recipient handles it under its own terms. Keep exported files private if they contain your gameplay history.

Your choices

You can play solo without Game Center, choose whether to enter a lobby, choose whether to share aggregate play statistics, and control Race Engineer in Settings. Removing the app removes its local app data but does not necessarily remove exported files or information stored by Game Center. Keychain items may remain on your device after app deletion; they are not a developer-held player account. Online records follow the retention periods above. Manage Game Center information through Apple's settings and services.
